Digital Humanities

Course Collage

This course collage features ten selected slides from class presentations that stood out to me most, shown in no particular order.

This slide describes the importance of memory and past experiences within fiction. By comparing memory to the now changed Mississippi River, it shows how natural it is to use memory when creating our own stories.

Comparing a book to an artifact, something of historical significance, was not something I had ever thought about. Anything, whether it is fiction or nonfiction, old or new, is an artifact to be studied within the humanities. It is human record to be analyzed within libraries or museums as an artifact of cultural production.

I liked this slide specifically because, as a media studies major, I always understood that no idea was original. Something always comes from something else. I enjoy how this slide explains the connections between man and the arts (and each other).

It is interesting to see the reactions of people online to ChatGPT. They’re upset with the way it is taking over the humanities and education as a whole.
